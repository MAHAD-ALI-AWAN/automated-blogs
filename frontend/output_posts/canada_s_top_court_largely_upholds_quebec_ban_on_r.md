## Detailed Analysis & Insights

Court rules against the ban

The Supreme Court of Canada ruled Thursday that the Quebec government's ban on the wearing of religious symbols on public-sector employees is constitutional.

The ruling came in a case that raised the issue of what's considered religious expression in a democracy.

The court said public servants have a right to wear religious symbols on their uniforms, such as turbans, niqabs and crucifixes.

But the law makes no mention of religious symbols on government-issued uniforms, according to the court.

The issue now before the courts concerns the Quebec government's plan to ban the wearing of religious symbols on public-sector employees, from the public service to judges and cabinet ministers.

The Quebec government says the ban violates freedom of religion and expression.

The case was brought by the Catholic diocese of Montreal and three religious groups. The groups argued that the law violates the Charter of Rights and Freedoms, which guarantees equal rights to religious minorities.
