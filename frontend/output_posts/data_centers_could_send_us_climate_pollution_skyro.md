A study from Climate Central says that while the US's carbon emissions in 2016 were about twice as much as the previous year's, the emissions from carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases in the atmosphere is only about 1.4 percent of the total.

The findings, written by the Climate Central team in conjunction with the US Energy Information Administration, found that emissions from carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, and other greenhouse gases were higher than usual in 2016, particularly in the western United States.

For the first time, the study also found that emissions from natural gas were up in 2016, as well as the emissions from coal-heavy power plants.

As the study pointed out, the US coal industry has seen a number of recent announcements about the need for cleaner coal. Two of the largest – Southern Company and Peabody Energy – recently declared that they will phase out coal-fired power plants by 2040 and 2020.
