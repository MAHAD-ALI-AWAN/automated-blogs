## Rivian partner Mind Robotics hires Doug Field as president

![Cover Image](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1518770660439-4636190af475?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

Rivian CEO RJ Scaringe has quietly built a robotics effort that appears to be turning from a strategic experiment into a more serious operating business. The latest move—appointing Doug Field as president of Mind Robotics—signals that the project is gaining experienced leadership and a sharper long-term focus.

## Why this hire matters

Doug Field brings heavy technical and product credibility to the startup. He has worked at the intersection of hardware, software, and product execution, which matters for a company trying to move from early concept work into real-world robotics deployment.

For Mind Robotics, the importance of the hire is not only the name. It is the signal that the project is becoming more structured, more deliberate, and more capable of competing in an environment where autonomy and AI increasingly matter.

## What Mind Robotics is trying to build

The project has remained relatively low-profile, but the direction is clear: build robotics and AI capabilities that could eventually affect transportation, logistics, and broader industrial systems. With Rivian's backing and access to engineering resources, the company has the potential to move faster than many early-stage efforts.

That is precisely why a leader like Doug Field matters. He brings experience navigating large, technically difficult programs, which is the kind of expertise a robotics startup needs as it tries to move beyond experimentation.

The comparison shows why the appointment matters: the space is crowded, and the difference between a promising idea and a real operating company often comes down to leadership, product clarity, and execution discipline.

> **Key takeaway:** Doug Field's appointment suggests Mind Robotics is moving from concept-stage ambition toward a more disciplined execution model.

## Broader market context

This is also a sign of a bigger shift in the technology market. Robotics and AI are no longer being viewed as isolated research efforts. They are increasingly seen as core systems for logistics, manufacturing, mobility, and productivity.

Companies are trying to build the operational muscle to turn advanced hardware products into real commercial tools. That puts leadership quality at the center of the story.

## Conclusion

Doug Field's arrival at Mind Robotics is more than a notable hire. It is a statement that the project is serious about scaling. In a field where execution often matters as much as technical vision, the combination of Rivian's backing and Field's experience could give the startup a meaningful advantage.

If the company can translate that leadership into disciplined product development, Mind Robotics could become a much more important player in the robotics landscape than its quiet start suggests.
