## Detailed Analysis & Insights

Nvidia said the move was to help it continue to expand its AI efforts in Southeast Asia, where it had invested $1.5 billion in 2015.

The decision came after several Chinese media reports said the company was testing a new AI chip called DeepMind, which is being developed by its chief artificial intelligence researcher, Demis Hassabis.

Nvidia said it was already using DeepMind as a testing ground for its AI chip, but did not disclose the names of the AI chip companies involved.

While the move may be a marketing ploy, Nvidia's AI chips in Southeast Asia are not used to make games or other software. Rather, they function as part of Nvidia's deep learning AI framework, which helps GPUs perform deep learning programs at the machine learning level.

In the past, AI developers might have used Nvidia chips to make games, but now they are using Nvidia chips to make AI programs that work across a huge number of devices, Nvidia said.
