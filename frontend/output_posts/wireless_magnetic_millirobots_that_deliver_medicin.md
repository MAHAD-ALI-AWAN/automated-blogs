A study performed by the University of Michigan in 2014 shows that a biomedical model can be built with the use of magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and light-emitting diodes (LEDs) to analyze the effects of magnetism on the heart. The team found that magnetism can influence the rate and duration of the beating, and can also cause damage to the cardiac muscle.

A new kind of magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) machine, the Electromagnetic Magnetic Resonance Machine, or EMMR, has been developed using the latest in electronic and digital technologies. The machine scans the heart using an array of four magnetic coils arranged in a grid. As the heart beats, the coils vibrate, producing an electric field. The magnetism produced in this process is a strong signal that can be used to track the electrical activity of an individual's heart.
